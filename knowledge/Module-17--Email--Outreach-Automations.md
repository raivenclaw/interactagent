# Module 17: Email & Outreach Automations

Email is the one channel nobody can take away from you.

Instagram can throttle your reach. Facebook can raise ad costs. Google can change the algorithm. TikTok can get banned. But your email list? That's yours. Every name on it opted in. Every message you send lands directly in their inbox. No algorithm. No bidding. No middleman.

The numbers back this up. Email marketing returns roughly $36 for every $1 spent. That's not a typo. No other channel comes close. And for ecommerce specifically, email drives more repeat purchases than every other channel combined.

Yet most ecom founders treat email as an afterthought. They set up one welcome email, maybe an abandoned cart reminder, and call it done. That's leaving thousands on the table every month.

This module fixes that. You're going to build complete email sequences for every stage of your customer journey -- and you're going to do it in a single session with Claude Code.

## 1. The Email Sequences Every Ecom Brand Needs

There are six types of email sequences that drive revenue for ecommerce businesses. You need all of them. Not eventually -- now.

1. **Welcome sequence** -- converts new subscribers into first-time buyers
2. **Abandoned cart sequence** -- recovers lost sales from people who almost bought
3. **Post-purchase sequence** -- turns one-time buyers into repeat customers and fans
4. **Win-back sequence** -- re-engages customers who went quiet
5. **Promotional sequence** -- drives revenue during sales, launches, and events
6. **Newsletter** -- keeps your brand top of mind between purchases

Most brands have one or two of these. The ones making real money have all six running simultaneously, automatically, every single day.

Think of it like a retail store. The welcome sequence is the greeting when someone walks in. The abandoned cart sequence is the sales associate who follows up when someone puts something back on the shelf. The post-purchase sequence is the thank-you note and the "you might also like" recommendation. The win-back is the "we haven't seen you in a while" phone call.

You wouldn't run a store with none of these. Don't run an online store without them either.

## 2. The Welcome Sequence -- 5 Emails That Convert Subscribers to Buyers

This is the most important sequence you'll build. It runs automatically for every new subscriber. By the end of five emails, they should understand your brand, trust your product, and have every reason to buy.

### Email 1: Welcome + Brand Story + First Purchase Offer (Immediate)

Sent the moment someone subscribes. This email has the highest open rate of any email you'll ever send -- typically 50-80%. Don't waste it.

**What it needs:**
- A warm welcome that makes them feel like they made the right choice
- Your brand story in 2-3 sentences (why you exist, not what you sell)
- A clear first purchase incentive (10-15% off, free shipping, or a gift)
- One single CTA button

**Prompt:**

> "Write a welcome email for [brand name]. We sell [product]. Our brand story is: [2-3 sentences about why you started]. Include a 15% off welcome offer with code WELCOME15. The tone should be [warm/bold/minimal/playful -- pick one]. Write the subject line, preview text, and full email body. End with one clear CTA button that says 'Shop Now'. Keep it under 200 words."

**A/B test variant prompt:**

> "Now write an alternative version of that welcome email. Same offer, same brand, but try a completely different subject line approach. Version A was [type -- e.g., direct]. Make version B [type -- e.g., curiosity-driven]. Also change the opening line."

### Email 2: Educational Content (Day 2)

The subscriber just met you. They're not ready for another sales pitch. This email builds authority by teaching them something useful about your niche.

**What it needs:**
- Valuable content related to your product category
- Position yourself as the expert
- Subtle product mention -- don't sell, just reference
- No discount code, no hard CTA

**Prompt:**

> "Write an educational email for [brand name]. We sell [product]. Our audience cares about [topic/problem]. Write an email that teaches them [specific useful thing -- e.g., '5 signs your skincare routine is damaging your skin' or 'How to style one jacket three ways']. The tone should feel like advice from a knowledgeable friend. Mention our product naturally once, but don't sell. Subject line should make them curious enough to open. Keep it under 250 words."

### Email 3: Social Proof (Day 4)

Trust is the bottleneck for first purchases. This email removes doubt by showing real people who already love your product.

**What it needs:**
- 3-5 real customer quotes (or realistic ones based on your actual reviews)
- Star ratings if you have them
- A photo or UGC reference
- CTA back to the product page

**Prompt:**

> "Write a social proof email for [brand name]. We sell [product]. Here are real customer reviews: [paste 5-10 reviews or summaries]. Pick the 3-4 most compelling quotes. Format them as a testimonial-style email with the customer's first name and star rating. Add a short intro that says something like 'Don't just take our word for it.' End with a CTA to shop. Include subject line and preview text."

**If you don't have reviews yet:**

> "Write a social proof email for [brand name]. We sell [product] and we're a newer brand. Instead of customer reviews, use these trust signals: [e.g., '500+ units sold in first month', 'Featured in [publication]', 'Made with [certification/ingredient]', '30-day money-back guarantee']. Format it as a trust-building email. Subject line, preview text, full body."

### Email 4: Product Showcase (Day 7)

By now they know your brand, trust your expertise, and have seen social proof. Time to show them what you sell.

**What it needs:**
- 3-4 bestselling products with short, benefit-focused descriptions
- Price for each product
- Individual product links
- The welcome offer reminder ("Your 15% off is still active")

**Prompt:**

> "Write a product showcase email for [brand name]. Feature these products: [list 3-4 products with name, price, and one key benefit each]. Write a short, punchy description for each product (2-3 sentences max, focus on what it does for the customer, not features). Remind them their 15% welcome code is still active. Include subject line and preview text. Format with clear visual breaks between products."

### Email 5: Urgency Close (Day 10)

The welcome offer is expiring. This is your last push. Make it count.

**What it needs:**
- Clear deadline -- "Your offer expires in 48 hours"
- Recap the offer one more time
- Remind them what they're missing
- Strong, urgent CTA
- Scarcity without being sleazy

**Prompt:**

> "Write a final welcome sequence email for [brand name]. The customer's 15% welcome offer (code WELCOME15) expires in 48 hours. Create urgency without being pushy or fake. Remind them what makes [product] special in one sentence. Include a clear deadline. CTA should feel like a last friendly nudge, not a hard sell. Subject line should create urgency. Preview text should reinforce the deadline. Keep it under 150 words -- short and punchy."

**A/B test for the urgency email:**

> "Write two subject line variants for this urgency email. Version A: straightforward deadline ('Your 15% off expires tomorrow'). Version B: loss aversion ('Don't let this go'). Which would you recommend and why?"

## 3. The Abandoned Cart Sequence -- 3 Emails That Recover Lost Sales

70% of online shopping carts get abandoned. That's not a problem -- that's an opportunity. These three emails typically recover 5-15% of abandoned carts. On a store doing $50K/month, that's $2,500-$7,500 in recovered revenue.

### Email 1: Gentle Reminder (1 Hour After Abandonment)

No pressure. No discount. Just a friendly "hey, you left something behind."

**Prompt:**

> "Write an abandoned cart email (email 1 of 3) for [brand name]. This goes out 1 hour after someone adds [product type] to their cart and leaves. Tone: helpful, not pushy. Show them the product they left behind (use a placeholder for the product image and name). Don't offer any discount yet. Just remind them it's still in their cart and make it easy to go back. Subject line should be casual and curious, not salesy. CTA: 'Complete Your Order'. Keep it under 100 words."

### Email 2: Social Proof + Benefits (24 Hours After Abandonment)

They didn't come back after the gentle reminder. Now give them a reason to. Add social proof and reinforce why this product is worth it.

**Prompt:**

> "Write abandoned cart email 2 of 3 for [brand name]. It's been 24 hours since they abandoned their cart with [product type]. This email should: (1) Show the product again (2) Add 2-3 short customer quotes about this product (3) List the top 3 benefits of the product -- not features, benefits. What does it DO for them? (4) Still no discount. We're saving that. Subject line should introduce social proof. Keep it under 200 words."

### Email 3: Final Reminder + Incentive (48 Hours After Abandonment)

Last email. Bring out the incentive.

**Prompt:**

> "Write the final abandoned cart email (3 of 3) for [brand name]. It's been 48 hours. The customer has been reminded once and shown social proof. Now offer a small incentive: [choose one: free shipping / 10% off / free gift with purchase]. Make it clear this is the last reminder. Create gentle urgency ('Your cart will be cleared soon'). Include the incentive code prominently. Subject line should highlight the incentive. CTA: 'Claim Your [Offer] & Complete Your Order'. Keep it under 150 words."

## 4. The Post-Purchase Sequence -- 5 Emails That Build Loyalty

The sale isn't the end. It's the beginning. These emails turn a one-time buyer into a repeat customer, a reviewer, and eventually a brand advocate.

### Email 1: Thank You + What to Expect (Immediately After Purchase)

**Prompt:**

> "Write a post-purchase thank you email for [brand name]. The customer just bought [product type]. Include: (1) A genuine thank you -- not corporate, human (2) What happens next -- shipping timeline, tracking info placeholder (3) How to reach support if they have questions (4) A line about the brand's mission or values. No upsell. No cross-sell. Just gratitude. Subject line should feel personal. Keep it under 150 words."

### Email 2: Product Care or Usage Tips (Day 3)

**Prompt:**

> "Write a product tips email for [brand name]. The customer received [product type] a few days ago. Write 3-5 practical tips for getting the most out of the product. This could be care instructions, styling ideas, usage tips, recipes -- whatever fits [product]. Tone: helpful expert. Include one line at the bottom like 'Questions? Just reply to this email.' Subject line should promise value. Keep it under 200 words."

### Email 3: Review Request (Day 7)

**Prompt:**

> "Write a review request email for [brand name]. The customer has had [product type] for a week. Ask them to leave a review. Make it easy -- provide a direct link placeholder. Explain why reviews matter ('It helps other customers like you make the right choice'). Don't bribe them. Keep it genuine. If you want, offer a small incentive ('Leave a review and get 10% off your next order'). Subject line should be direct and friendly. Keep it under 120 words."

### Email 4: Cross-Sell (Day 14)

**Prompt:**

> "Write a cross-sell email for [brand name]. The customer bought [product]. Recommend 2-3 related products that pair well with their purchase. For each recommendation, write one sentence explaining why it's a good companion to what they already bought. Don't be generic ('you might also like'). Be specific ('Since you got [product], you'll love [related product] because [reason]'). Subject line should feel like a personalized recommendation. Keep it under 200 words."

### Email 5: Loyalty Program Invitation (Day 21)

**Prompt:**

> "Write a loyalty program invitation email for [brand name]. The customer made their first purchase 3 weeks ago. Invite them to join our loyalty program. Benefits: [list 3-4 benefits -- e.g., earn points, early access to sales, birthday reward, free shipping threshold]. Make it feel exclusive, not like a mass email. Explain how it works in 3 simple steps. CTA: 'Join [Program Name]'. Subject line should make them feel special. Keep it under 200 words."

## 5. The Win-Back Sequence -- 3 Emails for Inactive Customers

Customers go quiet. It happens. These emails are for people who bought once (or more) but haven't been back in 60-90 days.

### Email 1: "We Miss You" + Personalized Picks (Day 60)

**Prompt:**

> "Write a win-back email (1 of 3) for [brand name]. The customer hasn't purchased in 60 days. Their last purchase was [product type]. Tone: warm, not desperate. Acknowledge it's been a while. Show them 3 products they might like based on their purchase history. Include a line like 'We've added some new things since your last visit.' No discount yet. Subject line should be personal and warm. Keep it under 150 words."

### Email 2: Exclusive Returning Customer Offer (Day 75)

**Prompt:**

> "Write win-back email 2 of 3 for [brand name]. The customer still hasn't returned. Offer an exclusive returning customer discount: [15% off / free shipping / buy one get one]. Frame it as a special offer just for them -- not a mass promotion. Mention one new product or collection they haven't seen. Subject line should highlight the exclusive offer. CTA: 'Welcome Back -- Shop Now'. Keep it under 150 words."

### Email 3: Last Chance (Day 90)

**Prompt:**

> "Write the final win-back email (3 of 3) for [brand name]. It's been 90 days. This is the last email before we stop emailing them (say this explicitly -- 'If we don't hear from you, we'll stop sending emails. No hard feelings.'). Restate the offer from email 2. Keep it short, honest, and respectful. Give them an easy way to stay on the list AND an easy way to leave. Subject line should feel final but not dramatic. Keep it under 120 words."

## 6. Cold Outreach -- B2B Email Sequences

If you're doing B2B (wholesale, partnerships, press, influencer outreach), cold email is still the most effective channel. But most cold emails are terrible. Here's how to write ones that actually get replies.

### The Research-First Approach

The biggest mistake in cold outreach: sending the same template to 500 people. The ones that work are the ones that show you actually know who you're writing to.

**Research prompt before writing any outreach:**

> "Visit [company website URL]. Tell me: (1) What they sell (2) Who their target audience seems to be (3) Any recent news, blog posts, or product launches (4) Something specific I can reference in my email to show I've done my homework. Keep it to bullet points."

### The 5-Email B2B Sequence

**Email 1: The Opener (Day 1)**

Reference something specific. Offer value. No ask.

**Prompt:**

> "Write a cold outreach email to [contact name] at [company]. I run [your brand], and I want to [propose partnership / pitch wholesale / offer collaboration]. Here's what I know about their company: [paste research from above]. Reference one specific thing about their business in the opening line. Explain what I do in one sentence. Explain why it's relevant to them in one sentence. End with a soft CTA -- 'Would it make sense to chat for 15 minutes?' No attachments. No links. No PDF pitch deck. Subject line should be short and specific to them. Keep the whole email under 100 words."

**Email 2: The Value Add (Day 3)**

**Prompt:**

> "Write follow-up email 2 for my cold outreach to [contact name] at [company]. Don't say 'just following up' -- that's lazy. Instead, share something valuable: a relevant case study, a data point, or an insight about their industry. Reference the first email briefly. Same soft CTA. Under 80 words."

**Email 3: The Social Proof (Day 7)**

**Prompt:**

> "Write follow-up email 3. Share a quick result or testimonial from a similar company you've worked with. If you don't have one, share a relevant metric from your own business. Keep it brief. Same CTA. Under 80 words."

**Email 4: The Direct Ask (Day 14)**

**Prompt:**

> "Write follow-up email 4. Be more direct this time. Acknowledge you've emailed a few times. Ask a simple yes/no question: 'Is this something [company] would be interested in?' Make it easy for them to say yes or no. Under 60 words."

**Email 5: The Breakup (Day 21)**

**Prompt:**

> "Write the final cold outreach email -- the breakup email. Say this is your last email. No guilt, no pressure. Leave the door open ('If the timing is ever right, I'm here'). Keep it classy and short. This email actually gets the highest reply rate because it removes pressure. Under 50 words."

## 8. Subject Line Testing at Scale

Subject lines make or break your emails. A great email with a bad subject line never gets read. Here's how to generate and test them in bulk.

**Generate 10 A/B test variants:**

> "I have this email: [paste full email]. Generate 10 subject line options. Give me a mix of approaches: (1) Two direct/descriptive (2) Two curiosity-based (3) Two benefit-led (4) Two urgency-driven (5) Two personalized (using the recipient's name). For each, explain in one sentence why it might work. Then rank your top 3."

**Test preview text combinations:**

> "For my top 3 subject lines above, write 2 preview text options each. The preview text should complement the subject line, not repeat it. Show me all 6 combinations."

**Analyze your past performance:**

> "Here are my last 10 email campaigns with their subject lines and open rates: [paste data]. What patterns do you see? Which types of subject lines perform best for my audience? What should I try more of? What should I stop doing?"

## 10. Quick Wins

These are standalone prompts you can use right now, no setup needed.

**Rewrite an email in a different tone:**

> "Rewrite this email in a more conversational tone. Make it sound like a friend texting, not a company emailing. Keep the same message and offer, just change the voice: [paste email]"

**Turn a blog post into a newsletter:**

> "Turn this blog post into a newsletter email. Keep the key points but cut the length in half. Add a subject line, preview text, and a CTA that links back to the full article: [paste blog post]"

**Write a flash sale email:**

> "Write a flash sale email for [brand name]. The sale is [X]% off everything for 24 hours. Start with urgency. Show 3 products at their sale prices. End with the deadline. Subject line should create FOMO without being clickbait. Under 150 words."

**Write a seasonal campaign:**

> "Write a [holiday/season] email campaign for [brand name]. We're running [offer details]. Reference the season naturally -- don't be cheesy. Focus on how our products fit into this time of year. Include subject line, preview text, body, and CTA. Under 200 words."

**Create a re-engagement email:**

> "Create a re-engagement email for customers who haven't purchased in 90 days. We sell [product type]. Don't beg them to come back. Instead, show them what's new -- 2 new products or collections they haven't seen. Add a 'welcome back' offer of [X% off]. Tone: confident, not desperate. Under 150 words."

## 11. Workshop Exercise: Build Your Complete Email System

This is your hands-on exercise. By the end, you'll have a full set of email sequences ready to load into your email platform.

### Step 1: Gather Your Inputs

Before you start, have this ready:
- Your brand name and one-paragraph description
- Your brand voice (pick 3 adjectives)
- Your welcome offer (e.g., "15% off with code WELCOME15")
- 5-10 customer reviews (if you have them)
- Your 3-4 bestselling products with prices
- Your shipping timeline

### Step 2: Generate All Sequences

Run these prompts in order:

1. **Welcome sequence** -- 5 emails using the prompts from section 2
2. **Abandoned cart sequence** -- 3 emails using the prompts from section 3
3. **Post-purchase sequence** -- 5 emails using the prompts from section 4
4. **Win-back sequence** -- 3 emails using the prompts from section 5

That's 16 emails. It sounds like a lot. With Claude, you'll have drafts for all of them in about 30 minutes.

### Step 3: Review and Refine

Read every email. For each one, ask yourself:
- Does this sound like my brand?
- Would I open this email?
- Is the CTA clear?
- Is it too long?

For anything that doesn't feel right:

> "Rewrite email [X] in the [sequence] sequence. It's too [formal/long/salesy/generic]. Make it more [specific direction]. Keep the same structure and offer."

### Step 4: Export and Load

Use the email sequence generator tool (section 7) or simply copy each email into your email platform. Set the timing. Turn on the automations. Done.

### Step 5: Set Up A/B Tests

For your welcome sequence email 1 and abandoned cart email 1 (highest volume), set up A/B tests on subject lines. Use the subject line generator (section 8) to create variants. Run them for 2 weeks, then keep the winner.
